MFD boards

The cockpit's MFD screens: display boards on USB (an Elecrow CrowPanel 7" with firmware/esp32-mfd). Flash a new board with the MFD firmware, tell each which MFD it is - it remembers - and check them with a test picture that loops.

Boards

PortBoardFirmwareIs MFD

Finding boards briefly opens every serial port.

A board fresh from Elecrow runs its demo and shows here as New board: Flash puts the MFD firmware on it (about 25 s, several boards at once) and it is found again as an MFD board, ready to be given its MFD. Flashing again keeps the MFD a board was given. Only ports with the board's CH340 USB chip are offered, and only an ESP32-S3 is written to.

BattleTech 3.0: any board found and given an MFD here shows the 3.0 cockpit's panel during a battle - its key labels lit as the game lights them, the message line, engine status and weapon readout - drawn by the bridge, which the battle starts with it.

Bridge


        
The bridge carries the game's MFD pictures to the boards while the pod runs: it opens the ports of the boards given an MFD above, and takes the pictures from the game when The game streams its MFD pictures is ticked. Finding boards, test pictures and recording stop it, as they need the same ports; Stop on the launch page stops it too. Its log is bridge.log beside the game.

Plasma sign

      
The orange dot-matrix sign on top of the pod, facing the room: the pilot's name, large if it is nine letters or fewer, and their rank above it - from Sergeant up between stars, a veteran seen across the room. Drawn here as the pod draws it: the game's own commands, the same font, the same dots - 128 x 32 for the centre's LED matrices (Settings, below).

      

        
Every rank

          

        

        
As the console seats the pilot (LOAD). The rank comes from their career on the console (Leaderboards), written for the pods to profiles\ranks.ini; a pilot promoted mid-session shows the new rank within a few seconds.

The LED sign
    

        

        

        
The wiring test, as the sign should show it: a frame round the edge, a line corner to corner from the top left, and each corner named. A half missing or doubled is a colour or address wire (R1/R2, A-D); a picture upside down or back to front is the panel's input end. Every dot lights the whole sign, for its brightness and its power supply. While the pod's game runs it has the sign; these work with the game closed.

      

      

        
Names and matches

        

        
In a match the game puts the pilot's place and score in two boxes under the name, every second or so - the name goes small then, just above them, to leave the boxes room, and each number sits in the middle of its box; after it, the sign goes dark until the next pilot is seated.

      

      

        
Settings

        

        
On a sign wider than the game's 112 dots, the game's picture is moved across to its middle - in the sign's window, and in the bytes the control port passes on to a real display - and the rank line has the room for more stars: one more each rank from Sergeant up, six for a General at 128.

        

        
Off, the sign is as the game draws it: the name, and the match's boxes. The sign's window itself is on the Displays page (Plasma sign), placed with the other cockpit windows. Save, then start the pod again.

      

    


    

      
Leaderboards

      
Every pilot's career, from the match records the pods write - score, kills and deaths as the battle report has them - and from BattleTech 3.0's battles (500 a kill). A pod sends each finished match to the console's launcher; ranks come from experience over a whole career, both games.

Join a battle on another PC

        
When another PC's launcher runs the battle with seats for other PCs, this PC's cockpit takes one: its address (that launcher's This PC page) and the seat it gave you. That PC's operator chooses your name and 'Mech, as the arcade's did. Its firewall has to let the battle in - Windows asks the first time.
